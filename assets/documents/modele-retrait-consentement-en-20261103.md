# Template — Withdrawal of consent (Québec private sector and federal), health variant (restriction of access) and commercial messages variant (CASL)

Status: template in the website's document library — workshop draft written on November 3, 2026, based on the register of legal bases of the same day. No publication, no sending.
Certainty rule: every provision cited below appears in the register of legal bases with the status “verified against the primary source” (text read on LégisQuébec or lois-laws.justice.gc.ca). Limit of the template: for general withdrawal, the register carries no response time limit of its own outside the commercial messages branch (CASL, 10 business days) — the document therefore lends no 30-day time limit to general withdrawal. Limit: General information, not legal advice.

## The main document — withdrawal of consent (as composed with the user)

[First name and last name of the person]
[Address]
[Email or telephone]

[Name of the organization]
Attention: person in charge of the protection of personal information
[Address or email of the organization, from the directory of holders]

In [city], on [date]

**Subject: Withdrawal of my consent to the use of my personal information**

Dear Sir or Madam,

I withdraw the consent I gave you for [the following purpose or purposes: describe the purposes concerned — e.g. marketing, profiling, sharing with partners] in relation to the personal information you hold about me, [consent given on or about: date or context of the collection, if known].

My consent was given for specific purposes: consent given for one purpose does not cover another purpose (Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, s. 14). At the time of collection, you were required to inform me of the possibility of withdrawing my consent (s. 8). [Federal regime: I may withdraw my consent at any time, subject to legal or contractual restrictions and reasonable notice; it is your responsibility to inform me of the implications of such withdrawal (Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act, Schedule 1, clause 4.3.8).]

I ask you to confirm to me in writing the receipt of this withdrawal, its effective date and its concrete implications for the use of my information. I understand that the withdrawal applies for the future and that it does not call into question uses already made under my consent.

[Signature or name]

---

**Legal basis for this request** *(block printed in the document — it is what gives the text its weight)*
- Being informed of the right of withdrawal — private sector, Québec: Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (CQLR, c. P-39.1), s. 8 — at the time of collection, the enterprise must inform the person in particular of the purposes, of the means, of their rights of access and rectification and of “the possibility of withdrawing his or her consent”; on request, it also informs them of the information collected, of the categories of persons who have access to it and of the period for which it will be kept.
- Specific consent: LRPSP, s. 14 — consent must be “clear, free and informed and be given for specific purposes”, requested for each purpose; it must be express for sensitive information. Consent given for one purpose does not cover another purpose.
- Withdrawing at any time — federal: Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (S.C. 2000, c. 5), Schedule 1, clause 4.3.8 — “An individual may withdraw consent at any time, subject to legal or contractual restrictions and reasonable notice. The organization shall inform the individual of the implications of such withdrawal.”
- Civil basis: Civil Code of Québec, art. 35, 2nd paragraph — “The privacy of a person may not be invaded without the consent of the person or without the invasion being authorized by law.” Consent is the condition of lawfulness; its withdrawal removes the basis for the invasion for the future.
Sources: statutes cited, texts read on LégisQuébec and lois-laws.justice.gc.ca, verified on November 3, 2026 (register of legal bases, §5).

## Health variant — restricting or refusing access to one's information (text composed with the user)

I request, in accordance with sections 7 and 8 of the Act respecting health and social services information (CQLR, c. R-22.1), [to restrict access to the following information: …, to only the following service providers or categories of service providers: …] [to refuse access to the following information: …, to the following person: …]. I express this will explicitly (section 9). I understand that these choices can never compromise my right to receive services (section 10).

**Legal basis — health variant** *(printed block)*
- Restrict: LRSS, s. 7 — restrict access to named service providers or categories of service providers, for identified information.
- Refuse: LRSS, s. 8 — refuse access to named persons (spouse or close relative in a grieving process, cause of death, solicitation by researchers, in particular).
- Form: LRSS, s. 9 — the will must be expressed explicitly. Protection: LRSS, s. 10 — these choices “can never compromise the right to receive services”.
Source: Act respecting health and social services information (CQLR, c. R-22.1), text read on LégisQuébec, verified on November 3, 2026 (register of legal bases, §5).

## Commercial messages variant — unsubscribing (text composed with the user, often a single action in the sender's interface)

I unsubscribe from your commercial electronic messages. The unsubscribe mechanism must be at no cost and usable by a practicable electronic means, and you must give effect to it without delay and, in any event, no later than ten business days, without any further action on my part (Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation, s. 11). I record the date of my unsubscribing: after that time limit, any subsequent sending is documented in my file. As a reminder: the burden of proving consent lies with the person who alleges it (s. 13).

**Legal basis — commercial messages variant** *(printed block)*
- Unsubscribing: Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (S.C. 2010, c. 23), s. 11(1) to (3) — unsubscribe mechanism at no cost, by a practicable electronic means; the sender must give effect to it “without delay, and in any event no later than 10 business days after the indication has been sent, without any further action being required on the part of the person who so indicated”.
- Burden of proving consent: CASL, s. 13 — the burden of proving consent lies with the person who alleges it.
Source: Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation, text read on lois-laws.justice.gc.ca, verified on November 3, 2026 (register of legal bases, §5).

## Construction notes (do not appear in the document)

- **Choices that lead to this template**: purpose = withdraw a consent; the routing chooses the branch — private-sector or federal organization (main document, with the paragraph of the applicable regime), health (LRSS restriction/refusal of access — this is not a “withdrawal” in the same sense: the pathway presents it as the action proper to the health regime), commercial messages (CASL unsubscribing, often accomplished in the sender's interface: the website then accompanies through the log rather than through a letter).
- **Tracking started at the declared sending**: CASL branch — deadline of 10 business days, calendar file (.ics) generated, and any message received after the deadline becomes a dated entry in the evidence log (this is the substance of the contravention log); main document and health variant — the date of sending and the written confirmation requested are recorded in the file, without any legal time limit of their own being invented.
- **Cross-cutting layer** (register of legal bases, §0): LCCJTI, ss. 5, 6, 11 and 17 — probative value of the composed document and of its transfer certificate.
- **What the template does not do**: it sends nothing (the user sends, rule 8); it promises no result; it does not claim that withdrawal retroactively erases past uses or that it is equivalent to a deletion of the information (deletion falls under rectification — Civil Code, art. 40: information that is “obsolete information or information not justified by the purpose of the file” — rectification template); no general response time limit is lent to withdrawal outside the CASL branch (limit of the register).
