**Legal basis of this request** *(block printed in the document — it is what gives the text its weight)*
- Illegality route: Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (CQLR, c. P-39.1), s. 28.1, first paragraph — the person “may require […] to cease disseminating that information or to de-index any hyperlink attached to his name” where the dissemination contravenes the law or an order of a court.
- Injury route: s. 28.1, second paragraph — three cumulative conditions: (1) “serious injury in relation to his right to the respect of his reputation or privacy”; (2) injury “clearly greater than the interest of the public in knowing the information or the interest of any person in expressing himself freely”; (3) the measure “does not exceed what is necessary for preventing the perpetuation of the injury”. Re-indexation follows this same route.
- Factors of assessment: s. 28.1, third paragraph — public figure or not; information concerning the person at the time the person is a minor; information up to date and accurate; sensitivity of the information; context of the dissemination; time elapsed; in criminal matters, pardon or restriction on the accessibility of records of the courts of justice.
- Procedure and attestation: ss. 30, 32 and 34 apply (written request, written response within 30 days, refusal with reasons); in case of acceptance, the person in charge attests in the written reply to the cessation, the de-indexation or the re-indexation.
- Interpretation (cited as an interpretation, not as the law): Commission d'accès à l'information, citizen page “Vos droits” [official English wording not retrieved — verify before use] — cessation targets the enterprises that disseminate the information (it is no longer public); de-indexation targets the enterprises that make it possible to find it on the Internet (it is no longer findable by a search by name); re-indexation makes it less findable.
- Reputation basis: Charter of human rights and freedoms (CQLR, c. C-12), s. 4 — “Every person has a right to the safeguard of his dignity, honour and reputation.”
- Recourse: ss. 42 and 43 — examination of a disagreement at the Commission d'accès à l'information, within 30 days of the refusal or of the expiry of the time limit.
Sources: Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (CQLR, c. P-39.1) and Charter of human rights and freedoms (CQLR, c. C-12), texts read on LégisQuébec; interpretation read on cai.gouv.qc.ca; verified on 3 November 2026 (register of legal bases, §4).

## Construction notes (do not appear in the document)

- **Choices that lead to this template**: purpose = cause a dissemination or a referencing to cease; the composer retains Route A or Route B depending on the boxes checked at the routing step (Route B requires the three conditions to be fulfilled one by one — the journey refuses to compose the letter if a condition remains empty, and says so); the recipient may be the disseminator (cessation) or the enterprise that makes it possible to find the information (de-indexation) — two distinct steps within the meaning of the Commission's interpretation, which the journey distinguishes from the choices onward.
- **Time limit started on the declared sending**: D+30, .ics calendar file generated (deadline, reminder, opening of the examination of a disagreement ss. 42/43), entry in the chained proof journal with the fingerprint of the composed document.
- **Cross-cutting layer** (register of legal bases, §0): LCCJTI, ss. 5, 6, 11 and 17 — probative value of the composed document and of its transfer certificate; captures of the dissemination, recorded in the user's file with their fingerprint, serve here as exhibits.
- **What the template does not do**: it sends nothing (the user sends, rule 8); it promises no result — Route B is a demanding test, and the journey presents it as such before composition; there is no federal or provincial equivalent outside Québec at the primary source read: for a holder outside Québec, the journey attaches the step to rectification or deletion (Civil Code, art. 40) or to a complaint, without dressing it up as “de-indexation”.
