# Template — Request for access to one's personal information (federal, PIPEDA)

Status: template in the website's document library — workshop draft prepared on November 3, 2026, based on the register of legal authorities of the same day. No publication, no sending.
Certainty rule: each provision cited below appears in the register of authorities with the status “verified against the primary source” (text read on laws-lois.justice.gc.ca); the federal Commissioner's bulletin is cited as an official interpretation, never as the Act. Limit printed in the template: PIPEDA as in force contains no right of portability — this template therefore includes no portability variant. Limit: General information, not legal advice.

**Legal basis for this request** *(block printed in the document — it is what gives the text its weight)*
- Right of access, use and third parties: Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (S.C. 2000, c. 5), Schedule 1, principle 4.9 — “Upon request, an individual shall be informed of the existence, use, and disclosure of his or her personal information and be given access to that information.” Principle 4.9 also provides for a response within a reasonable time and at minimal or no cost only.
- Time limit: s. 8(3) — “An organization shall respond to a request with due diligence and in any case not later than thirty days after receipt of the request.”
- Extension: s. 8(4) — extension of no more than 30 days under conditions, with notice to the applicant.
- Deemed refusal: s. 8(5) — “If the organization fails to respond within the time limit, the organization is deemed to have refused the request.”
- Controlled fees: s. 8(6) — “An organization may respond to an individual's request at a cost to the individual only if (a) the organization has informed the individual of the approximate cost; and (b) the individual has advised the organization that the request is not being withdrawn.”
- Interpretation (cited as an interpretation, not as the Act): Privacy Commissioner of Canada, *Interpretation Bulletin: Access to Personal Information*, “Fees” section — “Fees are not to be used by organizations to discourage requests; an organization should consider charging fees for processing a request only when the request is exceptional, and then only at minimal cost”; the bulletin itself states that it is not binding.
- Recourse: s. 11(3) — complaint to the Privacy Commissioner of Canada within six months of the refusal or of the expiry of the time limit (access); s. 14 — application to the Federal Court after the Commissioner's report, within one year.
- Limit: no right of portability exists in PIPEDA as in force on November 3, 2026 (the data mobility right appears only in Bill C-36, not adopted) — no request for a portable format is therefore presented here as a federal right.
Sources: Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (S.C. 2000, c. 5), text read on laws-lois.justice.gc.ca; interpretation bulletin read on priv.gc.ca; verified on November 3, 2026 (register of authorities, §1 and §3).

## Construction notes (do not appear in the document)

- **Choices leading to this template**: regime = federal (organization subject to PIPEDA: federally regulated business, or business in a province without a substantially similar Act in force for its sector); purpose = seeing one's data, knowing the use made of it and the third parties.
- **Time limit started on declared sending**: D+30 (response; extension of 30 days possible with notice), calendar .ics file generated (deadline, extended deadline if applicable, follow-up, opening of the six-month complaint window under s. 11(3)), entry in the chained proof log with the fingerprint of the composed document.
- **Cross-cutting layer** (register of authorities, §0): LCCJTI, ss. 5, 6, 11 and 17 — probative value of the composed document and of its transfer certificate.
- **What the template does not do**: it sends nothing (the user sends, rule 8); it promises no result; it contains no portability variant (absence established in the register — the standalone portability template tells the federal user this instead of producing a document without a basis); the Canadian Charter is not cited in it (it binds the State, not private organizations — s. 32).
