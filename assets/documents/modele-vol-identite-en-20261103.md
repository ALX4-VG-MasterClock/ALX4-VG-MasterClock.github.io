# Template — Identity theft pathway (Québec/Canada): ordered actions, to check off, integrated evidence log

Status: template in the website's document library — workshop draft written on November 3, 2026, based on the register of legal bases of the same day. This template is **not a letter**: it is a pathway-document that the user fills in as they carry out their steps. No publication, no sending.
Certainty rule: every provision cited below appears in the register of legal bases with the status “verified against the primary source” (text read on lois-laws.justice.gc.ca or on the official website of the authority); the Commission d'accès à l'information page on the security freeze is cited as an official interpretation (authority page describing the regime). Limit: General information, not legal advice; the telephone numbers below are those of the official pathways read on November 3, 2026 and must be re-verified against the official sources at each update of the website.
Planned composition on the website: local, in the visitor's browser; the fields in [brackets] are filled in with them; each step completed becomes a dated entry in the evidence log, chained by fingerprint to the previous one (design rules 2, 4 and 10).

### Step 6 — Evidence log (integrated into the pathway)
Each action above, and each action that follows (call, sending, response received, document transmitted), becomes an entry in this log: **date — time — action — person spoken to (name, organization) — file or reference number — what was said or obtained — next step**. The log is exported with the pathway; it is hosted nowhere other than with the user.

**Legal basis for this pathway** *(block printed in the document — it is what gives the text its weight)*
- Identity theft: Criminal Code, s. 402.2(1) — obtaining or having in one's possession another person's identity information in circumstances giving rise to a reasonable inference that it is intended to be used to commit an indictable offence that includes fraud, deceit or falsehood as an element of the offence; trafficking in subsection (2); maximum penalty of five years in subsection (5).
- Identity fraud (personation): Criminal Code, s. 403(1) — fraudulently personating another person, living or dead, in particular with intent to gain an advantage or obtain property; using another person's identity information “as if it pertains to the person using it” is personation (subsec. (2)); maximum penalty of ten years (subsec. (3)).
- Interception of private communications: Criminal Code, s. 184(1) — knowingly intercepting a private communication by means of a device is an indictable offence (five years maximum); exception for the express or implied consent of the originator or of the intended recipient (s. 184(2)(a)).
- Disclosure of an intercepted communication: Criminal Code, s. 193(1) — “every person commits an offence who, without the express consent of the originator of that communication or of the person intended to receive it, knowingly […] uses or discloses the private communication or any part of it or the substance, meaning or purpose of it or of any part of it”, or discloses its existence; penalty: two years maximum on indictment, or summary conviction procedure (subsec. (1.1)); exemptions in subsection (2).
- The Criminal Code does not confer a right to exercise oneself: it names exactly what happened to the victim, and that is what structures their reports.
- The actions, in the official order: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, page “What to do if you're a victim of fraud” — (1) gather the information; (2) financial institution (warning note on the accounts, passwords, report to Equifax and TransUnion); (3) local police (file number); (4) Anti-Fraud Centre, online or at 1-888-495-8501; (5) government agencies affected (CRA 1-800-959-8281; Service Canada 1-800-622-6232).
- Blocking credit — Québec (official interpretation, cited as such): Commission d'accès à l'information, page “Credit file” — since February 1, 2023, the security freeze: the credit assessment agent cannot communicate the information in the frozen file for the entering into of a credit contract, a lease contract and certain remote service contracts (Act respecting credit assessment agents).
Sources: Criminal Code (R.S.C.), texts read on lois-laws.justice.gc.ca; Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca; Commission d'accès à l'information, cai.gouv.qc.ca; verified on November 3, 2026 (register of legal bases, §8).

## Construction notes (do not appear in the document)

- **Choices that lead to this template**: situation = suspected identity theft or identity fraud; the pathway adapts to the compromised documents checked off at opening (step 5 and the sub-actions unfold according to the cases). The “check-off pathway” form takes up the FTC pattern rebuilt for Québec and Canada (idea 7 of the November 3 sweep): questions → ordered plan → actions → log.
- **Tracking**: no single legal time limit — tracking is by step (remaining actions always visible when the pathway is resumed, the progress being kept on the user's device, rule 12); the chained evidence log is the central piece: each entry carries the fingerprint of the previous one, and the pathway is exported at each step (rules 2, 4 and 10).
- **Cross-cutting layer** (register of legal bases, §0): LCCJTI, ss. 5, 6, 11 and 17 — probative value of the documents produced during the pathway (statement, letters to creditors composed locally on the same pattern as the other templates) and of the log itself.
- **What the template does not do**: it reports nothing in the user's place (each action is theirs, rule 8); it confers no official status — unlike the U.S. FTC's identity theft report, the plan generated here is an organized aide-mémoire, and the document carries that in its limit; it promises no result; the telephone numbers and the order of the actions must be re-verified against the official sources at each update of the website, and the date of verification appears in the page (certainty rule).
