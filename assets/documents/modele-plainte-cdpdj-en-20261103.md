**How this text is filed, and what follows** *(block printed with the document — mechanics verified on the Commission's official website, on 3 November 2026)*
- **Form**: a complaint to the Commission is made **in writing, using its online form** (plainte.cdpdj.qc.ca). It is **free**. The form must be **completed in a single session**: if you close it before clicking "Submit", the data is lost. That is why this text is prepared in full first — you then transcribe it in one go. An acknowledgment of receipt is sent to you by email; a PDF copy of your form can be requested from the reception service (plainte@cdpdj.qc.ca). Your evidence documents are sent later, to the person who takes charge of your file.
- **Help**: if you have difficulty filling out the form, the Commission can help you complete it by telephone and answer your questions (1 800 361-6477); in certain particular cases, it may propose other ways of filing. You can also be assisted by a person of your choice, or ask an organization to help you file a complaint.
- **Deadlines for filing**: file your complaint **as soon as possible**. For discrimination or harassment: less than 3 years after the facts — but **less than 6 months** if the incident involves an employee of a police service or of a municipality. The Commission may decide not to process a complaint if the last relevant facts date back more than two years (section 77 of the Charter). For exploitation: up to three years after becoming aware of the facts (extension possible if your condition prevents you from acting).
- **Processing**: reception and admissibility, then assessment (the respondent is notified and gives their version; you will be asked for your evidence). The Commission first offers **mediation**, a **voluntary and free** process to which both parties must consent. Without an agreement, there may be an **investigation**, then a decision by a committee of three commissioners on the investigation report (the "statement of facts", on which you will be able to comment). If the evidence is sufficient, the Commission proposes **corrective measures** (for example monetary compensation, reinstatement in a position or in housing). If the respondent does not comply with those recommendations, the case may be brought before the **Human Rights Tribunal**, and the Commission may then decide to represent you there **free of charge**. The Commission has set itself the objective of processing a complaint within an average time of 15 months. You may withdraw your complaint at any time; the information you give is treated confidentially; and filing a complaint is a right — no one may subject you to reprisals for having exercised it.
- **A deadline that protects you**: between the filing of your complaint and the notification of the closure of your file, the prescription (limitation period) of your civil remedy on the same facts is **suspended** — the time spent at the Commission does not count against you.

**Legal basis of this complaint** *(block printed in the document — it is what gives the text its weight)*
- Dignity: Charter of human rights and freedoms (CQLR c. C-12), s. 4: "Every person has a right to the safeguard of his dignity, honour and reputation."
- Private life: C-12, s. 5: "Every person has a right to respect for his private life." The Québec Charter applies between private persons — it can therefore be invoked against a business or a person, not only against the State (scope established in the research of 3 November 2026).
- Equality: C-12, s. 10 — full and equal recognition and exercise of rights and freedoms, without distinction, exclusion or preference based on one of the 14 grounds it enumerates. Harassment based on one of those grounds: s. 10.1. Exploitation of aged or handicapped persons: s. 48.
- Remedy: C-12, s. 49: "Any unlawful interference with any right or freedom recognized by this Charter entitles the victim to obtain the cessation of such interference and compensation for the moral or material prejudice resulting therefrom"; in the case of unlawful **and intentional** interference, the tribunal may in addition condemn its author to punitive damages. It is this remedy that the complaint to the Commission sets in motion: corrective measures at the outcome of the processing, and the Human Rights Tribunal if they are not complied with.
- Primacy, with its limit: C-12, s. 52 — no provision of any Act, even a subsequent one, may derogate from sections 1 to 38, except to the extent provided by those sections, unless that Act expressly states that it applies despite the Charter. **The shield of s. 52 covers ss. 1 to 38**: the rights invoked above (ss. 4, 5, 10, 10.1) are part of them; s. 49 (Chapter V) is not part of them — its weight is real but of another nature. In case of doubt on the interpretation of an Act, it is resolved in keeping with the Charter (s. 53).
- Institution: the Commission des droits de la personne et des droits de la jeunesse (Human Rights and Youth Commission) is the body that ensures respect for and promotion of the Charter (mission stated on the Commission's official website and in its complaint leaflet). Its power not to process a complaint whose last relevant facts date back more than two years derives from s. 77, as the Commission itself states it in its frequently asked questions.

C-12), text read on LégisQuébec, verified on 3 November 2026; complaint mechanics: official website of the Commission des droits de la personne et des droits de la jeunesse (cdpdj.qc.ca) — frequently asked questions on the handling of complaints; transcript of the video "Understanding the processing of a complaint of discrimination and harassment"; declaration of services to citizens; leaflet "Filing a complaint for discrimination" — pages read on 3 November 2026.

## Construction notes (do not appear in the document)

- **When this path is offered** (remedy stage of the support algorithm): the CDPDJ complaint is the **third escalation**, alongside the CAI (Québec's data laws) and the federal Privacy Commissioner (PIPEDA). It is offered in two configurations:
  - *Escalation*: when a path under the data laws (access refused, rectification ignored, dissemination maintained) also reveals an infringement of dignity, private life or equality by the holder — the Charter complaint adds a lever that the data laws do not have: the cessation and compensation of s. 49, with the Commission's investigation.
  - *Parallel path, from the outset*: when the main infringement targets dignity or private life by a private actor and does not first pass through a right under the data laws — publication or dissemination of personal information, humiliating profiling, discriminatory refusal of a service, of housing or of a job. The data path (de-indexing under s. 28.1 LRPSP, for example) and the Charter path can run at the same time; nothing in the official mechanics read makes them exclusive.
- **Complaint first or formal demand first** (relationship with the formal demand letter template based on s. 49, produced separately) — recommended order, based on the verified mechanics:
  - **The complaint first, by default.** Three mechanical reasons: (1) filing **suspends the prescription** of the civil remedy on the same facts until the closure of the file (verified, Commission FAQ) — filing early protects all the other paths; (2) the complaint is free, requires no lawyer, and it is the Commission that investigates; (3) the filing deadline can be very short (6 months if the respondent is an employee of a police service or of a municipality) — a formal demand must never cause that deadline to be missed.
  - **The formal demand first, as a targeted exception**: when the immediate need is the **cessation** of an ongoing infringement (a dissemination that continues every day) and the person wants a direct and rapid step before the long processing (average objective of 15 months). Even then: if the complaint filing deadline is close to its term, file the complaint first or at the same time.
  - [Not verified — do not assert] The effect of an extrajudicial formal demand on prescription has not been re-read at the primary source in this pass; the template does not rely on it and does not mention it. Only the suspension attached to the complaint is printed.
- **Deadlines started upon declared filing** (in the user's follow-up file): (a) before filing, the router computes the applicable term — 3 years after the facts (discrimination/harassment), 6 months (police/municipality), 3 years after awareness (exploitation) — and the s. 77 two-year flag; (b) after filing: acknowledgment of receipt expected (the Commission acknowledges receipt of electronic forms — its service target is 4 days at 90%, *service target, not a right*); (c) long follow-up milestone: average processing objective of 15 months announced by the Commission; entry in the chained evidence journal with the fingerprint of the composed text.
- **Form in a single session**: major construction consequence. The site cannot pre-fill the official form or deposit the text in it; the locally composed document is therefore designed as the full text, ready to be transcribed in one go, and the user is warned before opening the form. The PDF copy of the filed form can be obtained from the Commission (plainte@cdpdj.qc.ca) and filed in the follow-up file with its fingerprint.
- **Variants carried by the same pattern**: exploitation (victim, or report by a relative — the person reporting is not a party to the file and receives neither the summary nor the decision, according to the declaration of services; the document tells the reporting person this before composition); reprisals (fourth type accepted by the official form); complaint brought by an organization on behalf of a person or a group (power of attorney and, for an organization, written consent of the person represented).
- **What the template does not do**: it files nothing (the user transcribes and submits the official form themselves, rule 8); it guarantees neither admissibility, nor investigation, nor referral to the Tribunal — the Commission decides, and may refuse or cease to act for the reasons provided for by the Charter; it promises neither a processing time (the 15 months is an objective of the Commission, not a commitment toward a file) nor a corrective measure; it cites no section of the Charter governing the Human Rights Tribunal (not re-read at the primary source in this pass); it replaces neither the official form nor legal advice for the situations that require one (the Commission itself refers, after the closure of a file, to a civil law lawyer for the other remedies).
